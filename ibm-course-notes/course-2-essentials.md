## CIA Triad — With Simple Examples

| Word | Simple Meaning | Example of Attack | How to Protect |
|------|---------------|------------------|----------------|
| Confidentiality | Only right people see data | Data breach | Encryption |
| Integrity | Data is not changed by wrong person | Man-in-the-Middle attack | Hashing |
| Availability | System works when you need it | DDoS attack | Backups |

---

## Types of Attacks I Learned

- Malware: bad software — virus, ransomware, trojan, worm, spyware
- Phishing: fake email tricks you into clicking bad link or giving password
- Social Engineering: tricks a person without any hacking — just psychology
- Man-in-the-Middle: hacker sits between two people talking and reads messages
- DoS and DDoS: floods a system with traffic until it crashes
- Ransomware: locks all your files and demands money for the key
- SQL Injection: types bad code into a website form to attack the database

---

## Security Control Types

- Preventive controls: stop attacks before they happen — firewall, encryption
- Detective controls: notice an attack is happening — SIEM, IDS
- Corrective controls: fix things after an attack — backup restore, incident response

## Interview Answers I Prepared

**Q: What is the CIA Triad?**
Confidentiality means only authorised people can see data. Integrity means data is accurate and not changed by unauthorised people. Availability means the system works when people need it. Every security decision is based on protecting one or more of these three things.

**Q: What is the difference between a threat and a vulnerability?**
A vulnerability is a weakness — like a door with a broken lock. A threat is something that could use that weakness — like a burglar. Risk is when both exist together at the same time.
